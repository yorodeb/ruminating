# HackForge - Ideathon

## Overview

The *HackForge -- Ideathon* is an idea-generation and problem-solving event designed for Computer Science students.

The objective is to provide students with an environment where they can:

* Identify meaningful real-world problems.
* Explore problems through a Computer Science perspective.
* Develop innovative and technically feasible solutions.
* Practice problem decomposition and solution design.
* Collaborate with peers from different areas of interest.
* Present and defend their ideas clearly.

Rather than providing participants with completely open-ended problem
statements, the Ideathon will use a **structured framework** of broad Computer
Science domains and focused **insights**.

This structure provides enough guidance to help students get started while
still leaving substantial freedom for them to identify the problem and design
the solution themselves.

---

# Why an Ideathon?

Students often find it difficult to move from:

> "I want to build something"

to:

> "Here is a specific problem, here is who experiences it, and here is why my solution could help."

An overly open-ended Ideathon can therefore become difficult for participants, particularly students who have limited experience with problem identification and ideation.

At the same time, giving participants predefined problems and solutions removes much of the creativity that an Ideathon is intended to encourage.

The proposed format attempts to find a middle ground:

> **Provide direction without prescribing the solution.**

Participants will be given broad **umbrella themes**, each containing several **insights** representing interesting problem spaces.

Participants can then:

**Choose a domain → Explore an insight → Identify a problem → Design a solution → Present the idea**

---

# Core Concept

The Ideathon will have five umbrella themes:

1. **AI/ML**
2. **Cybersecurity**
3. **Developer Tooling**
4. **Blockchain**
5. **Open Innovation**

The first four themes will contain a set of **insights**.

An insight is **not a predefined project or problem statement**. Instead, it
represents an area in which participants can investigate problems.

The **problem and solution are determined by the participants**.

---

# Umbrella Themes

## AI/ML

This theme covers applications and systems involving Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning.

### Suggested Insights

* **AI for Personalization**
* **Explainable AI**
* **AI Reliability & Hallucination Detection**
* **AI for Education**
* **Multimodal Intelligence**
* **Small / Efficient AI**

These insights encourage participants to explore AI beyond simply building another chatbot or integrating an existing LLM API.

---

## Cybersecurity

This theme focuses on protecting systems, data, users and digital infrastructure.

### Suggested Insights

* **Phishing & Social Engineering**
* **Privacy by Design**
* **Software Supply Chain Security**
* **Security for Developers**
* **Security for Everyday Users**
* **Detecting Anomalous Behaviour**

The goal is to encourage participants to consider both technical security problems and the human factors involved in cybersecurity.

---

## Developer Tooling

This theme focuses on improving the tools, workflows and environments used by software developers.

### Suggested Insights

* **Understanding Code Changes**
* **Debugging**
* **Understanding Large Codebases**
* **Developer Feedback Loops**
* **API & Integration Tooling**
* **Making Developer Tools More Accessible**

This theme is intended to encourage participants to identify friction points in the software development process and explore ways of improving them.

For example:

> **Understanding Code Changes**

could lead to ideas involving semantic diffs, code-impact analysis, code review, regression detection, visualization, or other approaches.

---

## Blockchain

This theme focuses on problems where decentralization, verifiability, shared trust or distributed infrastructure could provide meaningful value.

### Suggested Insights

* **Digital Ownership & Provenance**
* **Decentralized Identity**
* **Trust Without a Central Authority**
* **Transparent & Verifiable Records**
* **Decentralized Coordination**
* **Blockchain Infrastructure**

The intention is not to encourage participants to add blockchain to an application simply because it is a current technology.

Instead, participants should be able to explain **why a blockchain-based or decentralized approach is appropriate for the problem they have identified.**

---

## Open Innovation

Open Innovation will intentionally have **no predefined insights**.

Participants choosing this category will have complete freedom to identify:

* The domain
* The problem
* The target users
* The technology
* The proposed solution

This category is intended for participants who already have a problem they want to explore or who want complete freedom in their ideation.

A simple description could be:

> **Open Innovation — No predefined problem space. Identify a problem worth solving and develop a solution around it.**

---

# Encouraging Meaningful Use of Technology

A major objective of the Ideathon should be to encourage **problem-first thinking** rather than technology-first thinking.

Participants should be encouraged to ask:

> **Why does this problem need this technology?**

For example, simply stating:

> "We are using blockchain because blockchain is secure."

It does not demonstrate that blockchain is appropriate for the problem.

Instead, participants should be able to explain the relationship between:
**Problem → Requirement → Technology → Solution**

The same principle applies to AI/ML.
Teams should be encouraged to consider whether AI is genuinely useful for their
problem rather than using AI simply because the theme permits it.

This helps shift the focus from:

> "What technology can we use?"

towards:

> **"What problem are we solving, and what technology is appropriate for solving it?"**

---

# Evaluation Criteria

Rather than evaluating projects purely on how much software was built, the evaluation should primarily assess the **quality of thinking behind the idea**.

Possible criteria include:

| Criteria                  | Description                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Problem Understanding** | How clearly does the team understand the problem?                   |
| **Problem Relevance**     | Is the problem meaningful to the intended users?                    |
| **Innovation**            | Does the proposed approach offer a meaningful or novel perspective? |
| **Technical Approach**    | Is the proposed technology appropriate for the problem?             |
| **Feasibility**           | Can the proposed solution realistically be developed?               |
| **Impact**                | What potential value could the solution provide?                    |
| **Clarity**               | How clearly can the team communicate the problem and solution?      |

The exact weightage can be finalized separately.

---

# Summary

The proposed Ideathon uses a **guided ideation framework** to balance structure and creativity.

Participants are provided with:

> **Umbrella Themes → Insights → Guiding Questions**

but are responsible for discovering:

> **Problem → Solution**

The five proposed umbrella themes are:

1. **AI/ML**
2. **Cybersecurity**
3. **Developer Tooling**
4. **Blockchain**
5. **Open Innovation**
The first four provide focused insights, while Open Innovation remains completely open-ended.

This allows the Ideathon to remain accessible to students who need some initial guidance while still giving experienced participants enough freedom to explore unconventional problems and solutions.
